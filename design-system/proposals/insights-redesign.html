Insights — redesign

Two directions on the same content model, day and night. Both fix the four things you flagged: nothing was tappable, the consistency grid repeated Reflections, the screen looked identical every day, and orange carried every pixel. Both are built to be screenshotted — that is now an explicit job of the screen, not a side effect.

What changes from the baseline

applies to both directions

Structural

  • One card becomes a sectioned scroll. Every section is its own tap target with a chevron and a detail sheet behind it — the record, the year, moods, hours, week, rites, themes, level.
  • The lifetime heatmap leaves. Reflections keeps it as a navigation device. Insights gets a month-by-month calendar instead: seven small months, tappable, using c.heat0–heat3 so density reads as density rather than a solid orange wall.
  • Orange is rationed. Numbers are c.ink; bars are ink at 10–36% opacity; c.accent marks exactly one datum per chart — the peak hour, the best weekday, the current level. Night keeps the AMOLED canvas, the hairline + 48dp sheen, and the brighter c.accentDeep.
  • Day-to-day variety. A rotating read at the top ("Sundays are your longest entries") changes as the data changes, so the screen is never the same twice.

New features proposed

  • The keepsake card — a portrait, share-shaped artefact rendered from the record and skinned by the user's sky and palette. Free skies included; Plus skies and themes drop into the same slot. One tap to share, one to reskin. This is the flauntable object.
  • Time scoping — This year · 2025 · All time. Lifetime numbers stay, but progress becomes comparable.
  • Milestones — the next three thresholds with distance to each, so the screen has a forward edge and not only a backward one.
  • Themes — tag frequency sized by count, tappable into a filtered Reflections search.
  • Rites — per-rite completion, the only place the app can show what the habit is made of.

A · Keepsake

poster first, then modular cards

The pitch

The screen opens on the artefact. A 4:5 portrait card, sky art full-bleed, the number set in the display face, a 12-month mood ribbon along the bottom edge and a quiet wordmark — sized and shaped for a story post. Everything below it is supporting evidence in the familiar card rhythm, so the tab still reads as Daily Rituals.

Why it wins: the thing worth sharing is a designed object, not a screenshot of a dashboard. The sky slot makes every palette and every Plus sky produce a visibly different card, which is the variety you asked for and a real reason to own more skies.

Trade-offs

400dp of hero before any data. Heavy accounts get their numbers below the fold — acceptable here, because the numbers are already summarised on the card. The poster needs an art pipeline: each sky must ship a portrait crop, and the composition has to survive both a bright day sky and an AMOLED night one.

Sky art shown as a placeholder — drop the real Tideline / Aurora / Sakura portrait crops into the slot.

B · Almanac

typographic, data-ink, no card chrome

The pitch

No cards. Hairline-divided blocks, each one a plain-language sentence with the chart that proves it directly underneath, charts running edge to edge. A year selector at the top scopes the whole page. The record is set as a single huge numeral, the way an almanac sets a year.

Why it wins: it is the least orange, most readable version, and it scales — adding a tenth section costs one divider. The shareable artefact is generated on demand from the strip near the top, so the screen itself stays about reading your life rather than posing it.

Trade-offs

Quieter. Less immediately impressive on first open, and it leans on copy quality: every block needs a true, well-written sentence, generated from real thresholds. Skies and themes only reach the header band and the generated card, so palette ownership is less visible here than in A.

Both directions share the same data model — the choice is presentation, not scope.
